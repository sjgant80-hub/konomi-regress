konomi-regress

Did the checkpoint get better — and where did it get worse?

Almost every model comparison in circulation is invalid: the rubric changed, or the corpus changed, or it compares one aggregate to another. This refuses those outright, pairs the movement item by item, and reports the axis that went backwards instead of averaging it away.

Inadmissible comparisons are refused

If the rubric fingerprint or the corpus address differs, there is no number to report. A caveat gets dropped on the way to the summary slide; a refusal does not.

Net is not the story — churn is

A net of +2 can be fifteen items up and thirteen down. That is instability, not progress, and it will not survive the next checkpoint. Both numbers are always shown.

"Up overall, down on safety" stays visible

The most dangerous pattern in evaluation is a headline that hides an axis going backwards. Aggregate reporting conceals it by construction. Here it is the first thing said.

Six illustrative scenarios against a fictional system. Each is a real shape you meet in evaluation work — including the three that look like progress and are not.

Admissibility

A run records the rubric's content fingerprint and a content address of its own corpus. Two runs that disagree on either are not comparable and compare() throws. The corpus check has an explicit opt-in escape (onIntersection) because comparing shared items is often what you want — and it reports exactly which items it dropped, because a comparison that silently narrows its own sample is the same failure wearing a different hat.

What the gate looks for

HoleMeaning
MASKEDThe headline improved while an axis went backwards. Aggregate reporting hides this by construction.
CHURNThe net is small next to the gross movement — instability reported as progress.
UNDERPOWEREDToo few items moved for the direction to beat a coin, by an exact two-sided sign test.
NARROWAlmost nothing moved — the corpus does not discriminate between these versions.
LOPSIDEDOne criterion accounts for most of the movement: a headline wearing a suite's clothes.
DROPPEDAn intersection comparison narrowed the sample. Always reported, never silent.
REGRESSIONA criterion lost ground on enough items to be real rather than noise.

Honest limits

  • The sign test treats items as independent. If your corpus holds ten paraphrases of one prompt they are not, and the p-value is optimistic. That is a property of your corpus and nothing here can detect it.
  • Tails are computed exactly in BigInt, then reported to 1e-12. A smaller tail comes back as 0 and says so rather than being dressed up as a tiny non-zero number.
  • clean means the comparison can be read at face value. It does not mean the model is fine — whether the movement is good is a judgement about the axes, and this has no opinion about that.
  • All figures on this page are illustrative, against a fictional system. Nothing here describes any real model.

Pure kernels, no dependencies, no network. Every kernel is mutation-gated in CI.
